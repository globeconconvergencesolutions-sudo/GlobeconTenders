PROCUREMENT INTELLIGENCE SYSTEM (PIS)

AI-Powered Procurement Discovery, Intelligence, Qualification,
Compliance and Bid Management Platform

1.  PROJECT OVERVIEW AND IDENTITY Build a production-ready Procurement
    Intelligence System (PIS) that continuously discovers, monitors,
    analyzes, classifies and matches procurement opportunities from a
    broad and continuously expanding network of procurement sources.

Official product name: Procurement Intelligence System Abbreviation: PIS
Product category: AI-powered procurement intelligence, qualification and
bid management platform. Tagline: Discover. Analyze. Qualify. Bid
Smarter.

Evolve the existing application rather than unnecessarily rebuilding it.
Inspect the existing codebase, interface, database, authentication, APIs
and implemented features first. Preserve working functionality, identify
reusable components and produce a brief implementation assessment. Do
not stop at a mock-up or plan: implement working features. Never present
simulated integrations or static sample data as live. Clearly identify
external credentials or configuration that remain pending.

CRITICAL SOURCE REQUIREMENT TenderYetu is ONE external procurement
source among many. It is not the product name, the exclusive source, or
the limit of system coverage. The system must discover, validate and
monitor an expanding network of sources. Do not automatically prioritize
TenderYetu over other sources unless configured by an administrator or
justified by user preferences.

2.  CORE PRODUCT VISION Build the system as an AI procurement analyst
    working continuously for each organization. It must discover
    sources, monitor them, collect notices, retrieve documents, extract
    requirements, classify opportunities, detect duplicates, match
    opportunities against company capabilities, assess qualification,
    identify missing evidence and capability gaps, analyze market
    demand, recommend next actions, manage the bid lifecycle, send
    alerts and maintain an auditable history.

Workflow: DISCOVER → COLLECT → DEEP SCAN → CLASSIFY → DEDUPLICATE →
MATCH → QUALIFY → IDENTIFY GAPS → DECIDE → MANAGE → LEARN

AI should reduce repetitive manual work while keeping users in control
of final decisions.

5.  MULTI-SOURCE PROCUREMENT DISCOVERY The platform must monitor a
    broad, continuously expanding network of procurement sources. Known
    sources are initial discovery seeds, not a closed list.

Source categories include: - National procurement portals - Government
ministries and departments - Government agencies and state
corporations - County and municipal governments - Public universities
and research institutions - International procurement portals - United
Nations agencies - Multilateral development banks - Bilateral
development partners - Donor-funded projects - NGOs and humanitarian
organizations - Infrastructure and development projects - Corporate
procurement and supplier portals - Publicly accessible
supplier-registration portals - Professional procurement platforms -
Other relevant public procurement websites discovered by AI

Examples of possible sources include TenderYetu, Kenya PPIP, UNGM, World
Bank procurement pages, UNDP procurement pages, African Development Bank
procurement pages, government advertising portals, county websites,
university procurement pages and other independently identified sources.
These are examples only, not an exhaustive list.

Use three complementary discovery methods: A. Configured sources:
authorized users can add websites and portals manually. B. AI-assisted
discovery: search for additional procurement portals, organization
procurement pages and relevant project websites. C. Organization-based
discovery: identify organizations that regularly procure services
matching selected fields and service lines, discover their official
procurement pages and recommend them for monitoring.

Search beyond “tender.” Recognize RFP, RFQ, EOI, ITB, IFB, RFI, calls
for proposals, consultancy opportunities, prequalification, framework
agreements, long-term agreements, supplier registration, contract
opportunities, procurement notices and procurement plans. Support
synonyms, local terminology and relevant languages where practical.

Search by meaning as well as exact keywords. For example, “Development
of an Integrated Information Management Platform” may relate to ICT &
Software, MIS Development, Systems Integration, Database Development and
Digital Transformation. Inspect descriptions and documents before
classifying.

Use appropriate combinations of public APIs, search-engine integrations,
RSS/Atom feeds, sitemaps, approved crawling, direct connectors,
authorized email ingestion and manual source registration. Respect
access controls, authentication, website terms, robots directives, rate
limits, privacy requirements and applicable law. Do not bypass security
mechanisms. Never claim complete coverage of the internet.

Distinguish discovered, approved and successfully monitored sources.

12. CLASSIFICATION AND SEMANTIC MATCHING Combine rules, structured

13. DUPLICATE INTELLIGENCE Detect duplicates using reference number,

    deadline changes and amendments. Show other publication sources,
    latest verified deadline, document history, duplicate confidence and
    conflicting data.

17. QUALIFICATION AND COMPLIANCE ENGINE Assess legal eligibility,
    registration, tax/statutory compliance, years of operation, similar
    experience, personnel, certifications/licenses, financial
    thresholds, technical capacity, geographic eligibility, JV
    conditions, mandatory documents and other stated conditions.

Display separate indicators for mandatory eligibility, technical,

confidence.

Overall statuses: - QUALIFICATION APPEARS SATISFIED - PARTIALLY
SATISFIED - QUALIFICATION UNCERTAIN - REQUIREMENTS NOT SATISFIED -
INSUFFICIENT INFORMATION

Base results on actual requirements and available evidence. A high
percentage must never conceal a failed mandatory criterion. Clearly
state that the AI assessment is preliminary and is not the procuring
organization’s official eligibility determination.

18. SEPARATE SCORES Never combine all assessments into one unexplained
    score.

Show: - Relevance: fit with selected service lines and capabilities. -
Qualification: extent to which requirements appear satisfied. - Evidence
confidence: completeness and reliability of supporting evidence. -
Deadline urgency: time remaining. - Source confidence: reliability and
currency of source information.

Example only: Relevance 94%; preliminary qualification 72%; evidence
confidence 88%; source confidence High; deadline 7 days. Actual scores
must use documented methods and available data. Do not invent scores.
Explain factors and display mandatory failures separately.

19. EXPLAINABILITY Every opportunity must show “Why this opportunity
    matches” and “Why this opportunity may not match.”

Matching explanations can include fields, service lines, capabilities,
projects, personnel, certifications and geographic experience.

Potential mismatch explanations include missing mandatory requirements,
insufficient documented experience, personnel/certification gaps,
financial thresholds not demonstrated, geographic restrictions, missing
documents or conflicting information.

For each issue, show the requirement, evidence reviewed and source
reference where available. Distinguish confirmed gaps from potential
gaps and unverified information.

20. AI RECOMMENDED ACTION Provide evidence-based decision support:

-   BID DIRECTLY — preliminary assessment shows no identified blocking
    requirement, subject to human review.
-   CLARIFICATION REQUIRED — essential information is unclear.
-   BID WITH PARTNER/JV — assess whether a permitted partnership could
    address gaps.
-   REVIEW REQUIRED — manual technical, financial or compliance review
    is needed.
-   DO NOT PURSUE — material conditions appear unsatisfied or the
    opportunity falls outside the organization’s strategy.
-   INSUFFICIENT INFORMATION — evidence is inadequate for a reliable
    assessment.

Show reasons, requirements, evidence, risks, missing information and
next steps. Do not automatically submit bids, sign declarations, contact
procuring entities or make binding commitments without explicit
authorization. The organization retains the final decision.

21. ORGANIZATION-WIDE CAPABILITY GAP ANALYSIS Analyze individual
    opportunities, service lines, fields, the overall company profile
    and historical opportunities.

Gap types: - Capability Gap: required technical capability is not
demonstrated or available. - Experience Gap: experience requirement is
not satisfied or documented. - Personnel Gap: required expertise is
unavailable or not evidenced. - Certification Gap: certification/license
is missing or unverified. - Financial Gap: financial threshold is not
demonstrated or appears unmet. - Geographic Gap: required local
presence, registration or experience is missing/unverified. -
Documentation Gap: evidence is missing, expired, incomplete or
unverified. - Partnership Gap: an eligible partner or consortium may
address a requirement.

For each gap show description, evidence, affected opportunities,
frequency, status (confirmed/potential/evidence-related), potential
corrective action, related service lines and review priority. Do not
infer lack of capability solely from an incomplete profile.

38. IMPLEMENTATION PHASES

Phase 1 — Assessment and Foundation: Inspect the existing application,
confirm the stack, establish navigation, authentication, organization
management, company profiles, fields/service lines, migrations and
access controls.

Phase 2 — Source Intelligence: Build the source registry, configurable
connectors, initial verified sources, source discovery candidates,
scheduled scanning, health monitoring and normalized ingestion.

Phase 3 — Opportunity Intelligence: Implement document
retrieval/processing, deep scan, requirement extraction, classification,
semantic matching, duplicate detection and detail pages.

Phase 4 — Qualification and Evidence: Implement Evidence Vault,
requirements matrix, evidence retrieval, qualification assessments,
confidence/provenance, gap analysis and recommended actions.

Phase 5 — Bid Management: Implement pipeline, tasks/collaboration,
deadline/amendment monitoring, alerts, document checklists and activity
history.

Phase 6 — Market Intelligence: Implement readiness dashboards, demand
analysis, potential upcoming opportunities, analytics and reports.
Improve discovery and extraction using measured performance.
